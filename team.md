*Group Leader / Lecturer in Computational Biology*  

After an initial training in statistical physics in Göttingen and Paris, Andreas worked on how evolution shapes immune defense strategies for his PhD at École Normale Supérieure in the [statistical biophysics group](https://sites.google.com/view/statbiophysens). As a Lewis-Sigler Theory Fellow at [Princeton University](https://lsi.princeton.edu/) he further developed his interest in evolutionary immunology and started to ask more practical questions around how to turn advances in measuring immunological processes into quantitative models. Combining theory and data analysis he uncovered quantitative principles that govern the regulation of T cell dynamics at multiple timescales.
